## Aim

To demonstrate how various prompting techniques (query decomposition, decision-making, semantic filtering, etc.) can be employed to create real-world engineering content — specifically an **educational report on renewable energy** and an **article on climate change** — using ChatGPT or similar models. The objective is to highlight how different prompt structures affect the content's quality, coherence, and structure within the Environmental Monitoring engineering domain.

---

## Procedure

### 1. Introduction to Prompt Patterns

The following prompt patterns were studied and applied:

- **Query Decomposition:** Breaking down complex queries into smaller, actionable parts.
- **Decision Making:** Asking the model to choose between options or directions.
- **Answer Engineering:** Refining outputs by giving detailed instructions on how to structure or format the answer.
- **Fact Check List:** Ensuring the content is factually accurate, especially for reports.
- **Tail Generation:** Extending the content logically to create depth in analysis.
- **Menu Actions:** Presenting multiple action choices and guiding the AI to select one.
- **Semantic Filter:** Applying filters to control the tone, style, and accuracy of the content.

### 2. Choosing the Content Type

Two content types were selected under the Environmental Monitoring domain:

| # | Scenario | Content Type |
|---|----------|--------------|
| 1 | Educational Report on Renewable Energy | Report |
| 2 | Article on Climate Change | Article |

### 3. Creating the Prompts

Prompts were started simple and gradually refined toward decision-making, tail generation, and semantic filtering techniques.

### 4. Generating and Refining Outputs

Each scenario went through: **Basic Prompt → Decomposed Prompt → Semantic-Filtered Prompt → Final Refined Prompt.**

### 5. Review and Evaluation

Outputs were evaluated for coherence, creativity/originality, factual accuracy, and tone/style match.

## Scenario 1: Educational Report on Renewable Energy

### Problem Statement

Create a well-structured, factually accurate educational report explaining renewable energy sources, their working principles, and their role in sustainable development — suitable for engineering students.

### Prompt Design

**Basic Prompt (First Draft):**
```
Write a report on renewable energy.
```

**Query-Decomposed Prompt:**
```
Break down the topic of renewable energy into the following sections and write
a short paragraph for each:
1. Definition and importance of renewable energy
2. Major types of renewable energy sources
3. Advantages and limitations of each source
4. Role of renewable energy in reducing carbon emissions
5. Future outlook and challenges in adoption
```

**Decision-Making Prompt:**
```
You have two options for the report's focus:
(a) A broad overview of all renewable energy types, or
(b) A deep-dive comparison of solar vs. wind energy for industrial use.
Choose the option best suited for an engineering student audience and justify
your choice in one sentence before proceeding with the report.
```

**Semantic-Filtered Prompt (Refined):**
```
Rewrite the renewable energy report in a formal, academic tone suitable for a
college engineering journal. Use technical terms (e.g., photovoltaic efficiency,
capacity factor, levelized cost of energy) but explain each briefly. Include a
short "Fact Check" note confirming that all statistics cited are general,
widely accepted figures (not overstated claims). Keep paragraphs concise
(4-6 sentences) and add a one-line conclusion summarizing the future outlook.
```

### Prompt Iteration

| Version | Technique Used | Change Observed |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| V1 – Basic | None | Generic, shallow overview lacking structure |
| V2 – Decomposed | Query Decomposition | Clear section-wise structure emerged |
| V3 – Decision-Making | Decision Making | Model justified scope choice, sharpened focus |
| V4 – Semantic Filtered | Semantic Filter + Fact Check | Formal tone, technical depth, verified factual framing |

### AI Output Evaluation

- **Coherence:** Improved significantly from V1 to V4 due to explicit section structuring.
- **Creativity/Originality:** Moderate — reports prioritize accuracy over creative flair, as expected.
- **Accuracy:** V4's fact-check instruction reduced risk of overstated/unsupported claims.
- **Tone and Style:** V4 matched the intended formal, academic tone required for engineering audiences.

### Ethical Considerations

- Renewable energy statistics can become outdated quickly; the report explicitly avoids citing specific unverifiable figures and instead frames data as "general estimates."
- Avoided promotional bias toward any single energy source (solar/wind/hydro) to maintain neutrality.
- Disclosed that the content was AI-assisted and should be cross-verified with peer-reviewed sources before academic submission.

## Scenario 2: Article on Climate Change

### Problem Statement

Generate an engaging, informative article on climate change that balances scientific accuracy with public readability, avoiding alarmist or dismissive framing.

### Prompt Design

**Basic Prompt (First Draft):**
```
Write an article about climate change.
```

**Query-Decomposed Prompt:**
```
Write an article on climate change covering these parts in order:
1. A hook opening describing a real-world observable effect
2. The core scientific cause (greenhouse gas emissions)
3. Two major global impacts (one environmental, one socio-economic)
4. Mitigation efforts currently underway
5. A hopeful, action-oriented closing
```

**Menu Actions Prompt:**
```
Choose ONE narrative angle for this climate change article from the menu below,
and state which you picked before writing:
[A] Data-driven scientific explainer
[B] Human-interest story following a community affected by climate change
[C] Policy-focused piece on global agreements and their effectiveness
Proceed with the chosen angle.
```

**Semantic-Filtered Prompt (Refined):**
```
Rewrite the climate change article in an engaging, journalistic tone
(similar to a science magazine feature) for a general adult audience.
Avoid overly technical jargon, avoid political bias, and avoid alarmist
language (no phrases like "irreversible catastrophe"). Keep the tone
fact-based but hopeful. Length: 400-500 words. End with one practical
action readers can take.
```

### Prompt Iteration

| Version | Technique Used | Change Observed |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| V1 – Basic | None | Generic, unfocused, mixed tone |
| V2 – Decomposed | Query Decomposition | Logical narrative flow with clear sections |
| V3 – Menu Actions | Menu Actions | Model selected a specific angle (human-interest), giving the piece a distinct identity |
| V4 – Semantic Filtered | Semantic Filter | Tone became balanced, non-alarmist, and audience-appropriate |

### AI Output Evaluation

- **Coherence:** Strong narrative arc achieved after decomposition and menu selection.
- **Creativity/Originality:** High — the human-interest angle produced a more original, less templated piece.
- **Accuracy:** Cross-checked against generally accepted climate science consensus; no exaggerated claims.
- **Tone and Style:** Matched the requested journalistic, hopeful, non-alarmist tone in the final version.

### Ethical Considerations

- Climate change is a sensitive and sometimes politically charged topic; the prompt explicitly instructed the model to avoid political bias and alarmist framing.
- Ensured the article did not misrepresent scientific consensus or present fringe claims as mainstream.
- Encouraged constructive, action-oriented framing rather than fear-based messaging to promote healthy public engagement with the topic.
